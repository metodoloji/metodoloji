## Findings

- **Finding 1 (design, accepted in review):** the budget is asymmetric on purpose. A symmetric bound (any skipped candidate ⇒ UNKNOWN, even after a match) would have been simpler but would throw away exact positives; a bound that answered `False` when candidates were skipped would invent a STALE. The shipped rule — a match inside the budget answers True, skipped-and-no-match answers None — is what the bench's two past-bound fixtures and the false-alarm control together pin.
- **Finding 2 (fixture integrity, resolved in review):** the bench plants its past-bound fixtures from the SHIPPED bound (`getattr`, with a declared floor of 4 MiB / 200 candidates), so a later widening of the constant cannot silently turn the fixture into a no-op; a shipped bound wider than the floor now fails the bench loudly instead of weakening the trial.
- **Finding 3 (known gap, recorded):** `bmad/scripts/workflow.py::_resolve_slug` resolves an omitted `--slug` through `list_runs`, so it now classifies every run to answer a question about slugs alone. The cost is bounded (bounded reads per run, and projects carry a handful of runs), and this iteration did not widen the record's scope to the CLI; a slug-only resolver is the cheapest next win.
- **Finding 4 (pre-existing, not caused by this change):** `sh scripts/check-plugin.sh` still reports the same 11 `UNVERIFIABLE` records (E-024 … E-034) it reported before this work — the documented machine-local key contract (README: "FORGED on a teammate's record is normal"). Audit §3b is green: every edited file sits inside an approved Code Scope, 33 scopes in force, E-053 reported STALE as superseded (report-only under `[hooks] freshness = warn`).
