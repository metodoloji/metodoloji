## Experiment: E-114 — The digest advises a roll-up from a ledger that is only a subset of the epic

- **Date:** 06.11.2026
- **Status:** completed
- **Theory:** An epic has two ledgers and only one of them is complete. `docs/planning/epics.md` holds the PLAN — every slice the epic promised, written or not. `docs/development/native/sprint-status.yaml` holds a SUBSET: its own SCOPE NOTE states that it lists only stories that already have a draft, and that epic statuses are nonetheless computed over the full epic plan. `state.sprint_summary` read the subset alone and handed `epic_progress` to two advice sites (`bmad/scripts/orient.py` next-steps and `state.format_sprint_line`), both of which fire when "every story is done" while the epic is still `in-progress`. On m-tesil EP-1 that was two listed stories, both done, against three planned slices — two never written. The digest therefore told the session "every story done — roll the epic up to done", a roll-up that would have claimed completion for work nobody had done. The failure is not a counting bug: a subset can never answer a question about the whole, and the advice asserted completion instead of naming the ledger it had counted.
- **Hypothesis:** H-114: "digest_epic_advice_accuracy >= 0.999"
- **Measurement Metrics:** digest_epic_advice_accuracy >= 0.999
- **Experiment Design:** Nine checks, each a statement a reader could act on wrongly. Fixture projects are built from a sprint file and an optional epic plan; the shipped `orient.build` and `state.format_sprint_line` are then asked what they say. The checks are: (1) a plan with more slices than the board must not produce "every story done" and must name the plan, its slice count and the plan→record mapping; (2) a plan that exists but does not exceed the board still routes through the mapping check rather than claiming completion; (3) with no plan on disk the advice names the sprint file as its only source; (4) a plan whose shape is unrecognised is reported as unknown, never as zero slices; (5) an epic marked done with a story still in progress keeps its own warning; (6) slice counting respects epic-section boundaries (`### S-` under `## EP-1` never counts toward EP-5); (7) an absent plan returns `{}`, not `{slices: 0}`; (8) the session-start line asks for the mapping when a plan exists; (9) that line keeps its plain roll-up phrasing when no plan exists. Deterministic, offline, stdlib only; no network, no clock, no board writes.
- **Sample Size n:** 9 checks
- **Census:** The nine checks enumerate every advice path the epic roll-up owns — the four digest branches (plan exceeds board, plan agrees, no plan, unreadable plan), the unchanged marked-done warning, the two counting behaviours the advice depends on (boundary and absence) and both session-line branches. They are the surface, not a sample of it; no sampling inference applies, and the control still has to fail the same claim.
- **Code Scope:** bmad/scripts/orient.py, hooks/engine/modules/state.py, scripts/bench/bench_orient_epic_advice.py, bmad/tests/test_orient.py, hooks/engine/tests/test_state.py, docs/experiments/E-114.md
- **Lineage:** E-046 (a board that could name work which does not exist — the same shape one ledger over: advice about work the reader cannot verify), E-113 (census framing for an enumeration of a shipped surface, and the announcement-vs-silence lesson this record continues)
