## Experiment: E-170 — A record's evidence is re-read at verify time

- **Date:** 2026-11-15
- **Status:** completed
- **Record Kind:** contract
- **Theory:** The gate stamps sha256= into 'Raw Sample Evidence' at approval, so every record SEALS its evidence — and no consumer ever re-read the seal. verify() checked the HMAC token and nothing else, so editing or deleting samples.jsonl after approval was invisible to every check in the tree. A bytes-only seal cannot simply be made fatal: 24 benches embed a live corpus count in a sample detail, so re-running one moves the bytes without touching the claim, and 19 records already drift for exactly that reason. The seal therefore gains verdicts=, a digest over each sample identity and its ok — stable across prose edits, broken by a flipped verdict — and the audit prints the drift it used to swallow.
- **Hypothesis:** H-170: "evidence_seal_accuracy == 1.0"
- **Measurement Metrics:** evidence_seal_accuracy == 1.0
- **Experiment Design:** Inputs: the gate, the audit and the bench population at this commit. Procedure: scripts/bench/bench_evidence_seal.py enumerates four probes derived from source, never a hand list (E-039). (1) verify() calls evidence_seal (AST). (2) A live count in a sample detail holds the seal while a flipped ok breaks it as VERDICT-DRIFT. (3) A deleted evidence file reports MISSING. (4) check-plugin.sh prints the drift instead of a bare VERIFIED. Control: --broken reads the PRE-FIX gate and audit from git HEAD~1 and must score below the threshold. Reproducibility: stdlib + ast + git, offline, deterministic.
