## Experiment: E-144 — The ledger CLI closes an entry instead of a hand edit

- **Date:** 2026-11-13
- **Status:** completed
- **Record Kind:** contract
- **Theory:** The deferred-work CLI could check, migrate and append, but closing an entry was a hand edit of status: plus a resolution: line. That is precisely the shape-memory cost the CLI exists to remove, and it had already been paid four times by hand: three entries closed in E-142 and one in E-143, each a chance to write a resolution: the parser cannot see, to forget the date, or to renumber an entry the format forbids renaming. The format defines two rules for a close - every close states its outcome, and an entry is never rewound - and neither was mechanical, so neither could be enforced. A ledger whose closure is unreliable cannot be trusted about what is open.
- **Hypothesis:** H-144: "ledger_close_accuracy == 1.0"
- **Measurement Metrics:** ledger_close_accuracy == 1.0
- **Experiment Design:** Inputs: the shipped tree at this commit. Procedure: scripts/bench/bench_ledger_close.py runs seven probes over temp ledgers - the surface exists, a close writes status: done plus resolution, the entry's number/origin/location/severity/reason survive the rewrite, a close without a resolution is refused with the file unchanged, re-closing is refused unless the resolution is byte-identical, a keep-open decision is refused at close, and the closed ledger still parses clean. Control: --broken asserts the absence of the pre-fix surface (there was no --close at all), so every claim fails; it scored 0.00. Reproducibility: stdlib only, deterministic, offline.
